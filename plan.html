Golden rule

The reveal happens only after the main guest is seated and before all speeches; if they are late, a ‘hold’ cue is given and the music and the city building itself on the LED carry on. Movement starts on the stage manager’s call, never on an official’s action. A back-up tractor stands ready behind the mountain.

Two fallbacks

If the venue refuses movement in advance: the tractor is parked inside the tunnel before guests arrive and the reveal happens as the tunnel lights up and the city turns green. If the tractor has a fault after guests are seated: the tunnel lights up, the city turns green, and recorded footage of the same move plays, clearly labelled 'recorded' — never presented as live.

Layout and guest flow

In through one city.
Out through another.

Guests never walk back the way they came: the grey city on arrival, the runway hall for the reveal, a separate route to dinner, and a second, living city on the way out. The layout is indicative and will be fitted to the chosen venue.

Guest flow

  1. Check-in in the foyer outside the hall; the hall doors are closed.
  2. The doors open and guests walk through the walled corridor into the city.
  3. Standing reception in the city street, among the shops and performances.
  4. Through two hidden doors in the LED wall into the runway hall and the swivel chairs; south-row guests cross the road before the show.
  5. The show and the reveal; then speeches facing the stage.
  6. Through two doors on the west side of the runway hall, straight to the dinner hall (‘Project Oxygen’); no return through the grey street.
  7. After dinner, out through the second city: a smaller hall full of flowers, vines and trees, with a group photo booth on the way and the gifts at its end.

Zones

  • Check-in foyer: outside the hall, in front of the main doors
  • Corridor: about 8 m, grey walls on both sides
  • Entry city (the grey street): about 80 m long, catering shops on both sides
  • Runway hall: about 60 × 27 m; road, 500 seats, mountain and tunnel, stage
  • Dinner hall: about 24 × 26 m for 500 guests
  • Exit city: a second hall, about 58 × 11 m, full of flowers and trees; photo booth, gift counter and exit doors
  • Catering back-of-house behind the shops; kitchen beside the dinner hall

Practical notes

  • Four hidden doors in the LED wall: two from the entry city to the seats and two on the west side to the dinner hall; all open from inside and are closed before the show.
  • The tractor comes in through the loading door and waits behind the mountain; its route is fully separate from guests.
  • The hall’s columns are hidden inside the facades and behind the walls.
  • This layout is indicative and will be fitted to the chosen venue’s plan; the loading door, ceiling height and floor load are confirmed then.
  • Emergency exits stay clear in every zone.
Materials and execution

How we build it.

Check-in foyer (outside the hall)
  • Counter: about 12 m in two sections, MDF with a matte graphite finish and a warm LED strip under the top; eight check-in points with tablets.
  • Back wall: dark graphite panels on a frame; the hall doors stay closed until check-in ends.
  • Floor: glossy black flooring or dark carpet.
  • Light: warm and indirect, spots on the counter.
  • Coloured entry cards split guests into the two row groups.
The walled corridor
  • Size: about 8 m long, 4 m wide, 5 m walls.
  • Build: timber or steel-profile frame clad in particleboard; aged grey patina paint with stains and soot.
  • Details: fuse boxes, meters and dummy wiring (dead cable).
  • Light and sound: low and cool with light haze; soft traffic sound; warm light and neon at the far end.
City street and shops
  • Facades: 6–8 m buildings in particleboard on scaffold or steel, light grey with an aged plaster texture; metal balconies, AC units and decorative shutters.
  • Shops: open ground floors 3 m deep — pastry, coffee, tea house, finger food, juice — with real counters, displays and equipment; catering support behind them.
  • Signs are neon icons only, no text; pink, cyan and violet neon-flex on balconies and windows; dead overhead wires.
  • Light: cold sodium light, soot gobos and light haze, with the coloured neon; this street’s light stays the same all night.
LED wall and cubes
  • One U-shaped wall from both sides of the mountain round behind the stage; about 125 m long and about 4 m high from the floor, just above the tractor’s roof (3.2 m). No cuts or black panels.
  • Spec: indoor LED, about 3.9 mm pitch, on a braced ground frame; two hidden doors for the rows.
  • Content: a modern holographic city of translucent cyan-white towers on deep navy, in four phases — building, a truck of light and GREAT WALL, turning green, the mammoth herd. No logo. One continuous file on a media server, timecoded with light and sound.
  • Cubes: four large cubes of about 3 × 3 m in LED cabinets, one behind the other in a single line over the road’s centre line, at about 6 m; hung from the truss on chain hoists.
Origami mountain, tunnel and brass band
  • Mountain: steel frame with folded, faceted panels in MDF or aluminium composite, matte light grey; 8–10 m high; rock texture mapped onto the facets.
  • Tunnel: round portal of about 6 m (at least 1 m taller than the tractor); inside, rings of LED light that come on before the tractor exits and fill the tunnel with light; the tractor waits behind the mountain.
  • Nobody stands on the mountain; it is only the empty, lit faceted surface.
  • Brass band: saxophone, clarinet, trumpet and trombone, with a male singer on a wireless microphone; once the tractor is out, they enter from both sides of the road and perform in front of it.
  • Haze: hazers so the headlights read in the tunnel.
Road, seating, stage, light and sound
  • Road: 6 m wide, asphalt-look flooring with white markings; low LED bollards along both edges.
  • Seating: 500 runway-style swivel chairs on tiered platforms.
  • Stage: about 14 × 6 m, 60 cm high, directly opposite the tunnel; behind it only the continuous LED wall; cool light from below (LED strip along the stage edge) and from above (moving beams).
  • Light: roof truss with moving heads, washes and spots; timecode control.
  • Sound: line arrays on both sides and speakers behind the mountain for the approaching diesel; silence at the exit.
  • Tractor: no trailer, on electric, walking pace, a guide in front and a stop mark.
The dinner hall
  • A forest LED wall all around: one continuous image of tall trees, mist and sunlight.
  • Real trees: 6–8 mature trees, 5–6 m, in planters, hired from a nursery.
  • Ceiling: real branches, vines and ferns on a net under the truss, with warm LED strings.
  • Buffet: about 20 m down the centre, natural timber, brass and copper, moss and ferns.
  • Round tables: light linen; low forest centrepieces of moss, stones, ferns and white wild flowers; no candles.
  • The exit city: a second, smaller hall with facades like the first; dressed from the start with flowers, vines on the balconies, trees in planters and warm string lights; a group photo booth with instant prints mid-street, and the gift counter by the exit doors.
Build and install schedule
  • Weeks 1–2: shop drawings, 3D model, LED and mapping content.
  • Weeks 3–5: off-site build of facades, mountain, counters and shops.
  • 6 days before: move-in, truss and lighting, floor and road.
  • 4 days before: LED and cubes, mountain and tunnel, the facades of both cities.
  • 3 days before: seating, stage, dinner hall, and the exit city’s flowers and trees.
  • 2 days before: light and content programming, tunnel lights and LED sync tests.
  • 1 day before: full rehearsal with the real tractor, the brass band, the singer and the host.
Safety and permits
  • All particleboard, timber, fabric and branches fire-treated.
  • Haze only in coordination with the fire service and the alarm system.
  • Hanging loads (cubes and branches) calculated by a structural engineer.
  • The tractor’s route kept a safe distance from the front row; a guide in front; a back-up tractor behind the mountain.
  • The brass band steps onto the road only after the tractor has fully stopped.
  • LED doors open from inside; emergency exits kept clear.
  • Confirmed after the site visit: ceiling height, floor load, loading door, power capacity.
