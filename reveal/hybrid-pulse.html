How The Hybrid Pulse is built

The amphitheatre

A round hall, like the theatres of the ancient world. One half is tiered seating for 500 guests, rising towards the back so every row sees the floor; the other half is one curved LED wall. Between them the floor is open — no platform, nothing on it but the tractor’s stop mark at the centre and a slim lectern that stands beside the mark from the start.

The LED wall and the gate

One continuous half-circle of LED, about 8 m high, with no cuts and no black frame. At its centre a section about 6 m wide slides open in two leaves; when it is closed the picture runs across it without a seam. The tractor waits behind it. The content is one timecoded file: an empty desert road, a truck approaching with dust behind it, its headlights filling the wall, then the blue holographic city. Everything stays on the screen — no light or haze is thrown at the audience before the gate opens.

The reveal

The hall dims and the desert holds. A truck appears far away and the sound of a diesel road grows around the seats. As its headlights fill the wall the sound cuts; the gate slides open and the real tractor, without a trailer, rolls out on electric power at walking pace to its mark in the centre. The gate closes, the desert becomes the holographic city, and the presenter steps to the lectern. Speeches and the official photo follow — the tractor at the centre of the blue city.
