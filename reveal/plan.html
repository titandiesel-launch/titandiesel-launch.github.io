The plan

Four hours, five minutes that matter

The run of show from 18:30 to 22:30, the guest flow, and how every space is built.

Run of show

  1. 18:30

    The Grey City

    Check-in; standing reception, shops and performances

  2. 19:40

    Seating and protocol

    Into the amphitheatre; protocol after the main guest is seated

  3. 20:00

    The desert

    Hall dims; an empty desert road on the curved LED wall

  4. 20:03

    Out of the desert

    A truck approaches on the LED; its headlights fill the wall

  5. 20:05

    The electric reveal

    The gate slides open; the tractor rolls out in silence to the centre; the gate closes

  6. 20:06

    The city wakes up

    The desert becomes the blue holographic city

  7. 20:10

    Speeches

    Presenter at the lectern; CEO, engineering, official; official photo

  8. 20:45

    Project Oxygen

    A separate route to the dinner hall; dinner in the forest

  9. 22:00

    Life Again

    Group photo and gifts; out along the living walls; 22:30 close

Golden rule

The reveal happens only after the main guest is seated and before all speeches; if they are late, a ‘hold’ cue is given and the desert simply holds on the wall. The gate opens on the stage manager’s call, never on an official’s action. A back-up tractor stands ready behind the gate.

Two fallbacks

If the venue refuses movement with guests present: the tractor is parked just behind the gate before guests arrive, and the gate opens on it standing in light as the desert turns into the city. If the tractor has a fault after guests are seated: the back-up tractor behind the gate makes the same move. The movement is never replaced by animation.

In through one city, out along another street

Guests never walk back the way they came: the grey city on arrival, the amphitheatre for the reveal, a separate route to dinner, and a street of living walls on the way out. The layout is indicative and will be fitted to the chosen venue.

Guest flow

  1. Check-in in the foyer outside the hall; the hall doors are closed.
  2. The doors open and guests walk through the walled corridor into the city.
  3. Standing reception in the city street, among the shops and performances.
  4. Through two doors into the amphitheatre; guests take their seats in the tiered half-circle.
  5. The show and the reveal; then the speeches, facing the tractor and the lectern.
  6. Through two doors at the side of the amphitheatre, straight to the dinner hall (‘Project Oxygen’); no return through the grey street.
  7. After dinner: the group photo booth and the gift counter, then out along the corridor of living walls.

Zones

  • Check-in foyer: outside the hall, in front of the main doors
  • Corridor: about 8 m, grey walls on both sides
  • Entry city (the grey street): about 80 m long, catering shops on both sides
  • Amphitheatre: a round hall about 40 m across — 500 tiered seats in one half, a curved LED wall of about 63 m in the other, an open floor in the centre
  • Dinner hall: about 24 × 26 m for 500 guests
  • Exit corridor: about 40 × 8 m, interactive LED walls on both sides; photo booth and gift counter at its start
  • Catering back-of-house behind the shops; kitchen beside the dinner hall

Practical notes

  • The tractor comes in through the loading door and waits behind the LED gate; its route never crosses the guests’.
  • The gate is the only opening in the curved wall the audience sees; guest doors are at the ends of the seating.
  • The hall’s columns are hidden inside the facades and behind the walls.
  • This layout is indicative and will be fitted to the chosen venue’s plan; the loading door, ceiling height and floor load are confirmed then.
  • Emergency exits stay clear in every zone.

How we build it

Check-in foyer (outside the hall)

  • Counter: about 12 m in two sections, MDF with a matte graphite finish and a warm LED strip under the top; eight check-in points with tablets.
  • Back wall: dark graphite panels on a frame; the hall doors stay closed until check-in ends.
  • Floor: glossy black flooring or dark carpet.
  • Light: warm and indirect, spots on the counter.
  • Coloured entry cards split guests into the two row groups.

The walled corridor

  • Size: about 8 m long, 4 m wide, 5 m walls.
  • Build: timber or steel-profile frame clad in particleboard; aged grey patina paint with stains and soot.
  • Details: fuse boxes, meters and dummy wiring (dead cable).
  • Light and sound: low and cool with light haze; soft traffic sound; warm light and neon at the far end.

City street and shops

  • Facades: 6–8 m buildings in particleboard on scaffold or steel, light grey with an aged plaster texture; metal balconies, AC units and decorative shutters.
  • Shops: open ground floors 3 m deep — pastry, coffee, tea house, finger food, juice — with real counters, displays and equipment; catering support behind them.
  • Signs are neon icons only, no text; pink, cyan and violet neon-flex on balconies and windows; dead overhead wires.
  • Light: cold sodium light, soot gobos and light haze, with the coloured neon; this street’s light stays the same all night.

Curved LED wall and gate

  • One continuous half-circle of LED, about 63 m long and about 8 m high; no cuts, no black frame.
  • Spec: indoor LED, about 3.9 mm pitch, on a braced ground frame.
  • Gate: a centre section about 6 m wide and 5 m high on motorised sliding tracks, opening in two leaves; the picture runs across it seamlessly when closed. Safety sensors and a manual stop.
  • Content: one timecoded file on a media server — empty desert road, the truck approaching, headlights filling the wall, the blue holographic city. No logo.

Seating and floor

  • 500 seats on tiered platforms in a half-circle, rising about 3–4 m to the back, with steps, handrails and aisle lighting.
  • Floor: dark, polished concrete-look surface; the tractor’s stop mark at the centre.
  • A slim lectern stands on the floor beside the mark from the start of the show.
  • A circular truss above the floor carries light and sound.

Light, sound and the tractor

  • Light: moving heads, washes and spots on the circular truss; timecode control. Haze only behind the gate, so the opening glows when it slides apart.
  • Sound: line arrays around the seating; the diesel road approaches with the truck on the screen and cuts the moment the gate opens.
  • Tractor: no trailer, on electric, walking pace, a guide in front and a stop mark.
  • A back-up tractor waits behind the gate.

The dinner hall

  • A forest LED wall all around: one continuous image of tall trees, mist and sunlight.
  • Real trees: 6–8 mature trees, 5–6 m, in planters, hired from a nursery.
  • Ceiling: real branches, vines and ferns on a net under the truss, with warm LED strings.
  • Buffet: about 20 m down the centre, natural timber, brass and copper, moss and ferns.
  • Round tables: light linen; low forest centrepieces of moss, stones, ferns and white wild flowers; no candles.
  • The way out: a corridor of about 40 m with interactive LED walls on both sides, about 5 m high, showing a real residential street; touch sensors at hand height and a real-time engine for the effects. A group photo booth with instant prints and the gift counter at its start.

Build and install schedule

  • Weeks 1–2: shop drawings, 3D model, LED content (desert, truck, city) and the interactive wall software.
  • Weeks 3–5: off-site build of facades, seating platforms, counters and shops; the gate mechanism tested in the workshop.
  • 6 days before: move-in, truss and lighting, floor.
  • 4 days before: the curved LED wall and gate, the city facades, the corridor’s LED walls.
  • 3 days before: seating, dinner hall, photo booth.
  • 2 days before: light and content programming, gate and LED sync tests, touch-wall calibration.
  • 1 day before: full rehearsal with the real tractor and the host.

Safety and permits

  • All particleboard, timber, fabric and branches fire-treated.
  • Haze only in coordination with the fire service and the alarm system.
  • Hanging loads (truss, branches) and the LED wall structure calculated by a structural engineer.
  • The gate has safety sensors and a manual stop; it opens only on the stage manager’s call.
  • The tractor’s route kept a safe distance from the front row; a guide in front; a back-up tractor behind the gate.
  • Touch walls: low-voltage, with a protective surface.
  • Emergency exits kept clear. Confirmed after the site visit: ceiling height, floor load, loading door, power capacity.
